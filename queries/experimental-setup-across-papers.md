# Experimental setup & evaluation protocol across the wiki

Query: how do the papers in this wiki design their experiments: how much data, what train/test split, how many seeds, which checkpoint gets reported, which metrics, which statistics? And what should my own protocol be? Right now I train 5 seeds and evaluate only the best one.

Sources: the experimental sections of every PDF in `raw/papers/`, re-read for this query. Three of those PDFs are **not yet ingested**: GPLight (`raw/papers/GPLight.pdf`), MPLight (`raw/papers/the MPLight paper.pdf`) and the right-turn-on-red capacity study (`raw/papers/Quantifying the impacts of right-turn-on-red.pdf`). They are cited by filename below.

## 0. Vocabulary: "runs" means three different things

Papers write "5 runs" or "± std" without saying which source of randomness they mean. You need to keep these apart, both when reading papers and in your own design. *(synthesis)*

| Randomness source | What changes | Called | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Training seed** | NN initialisation, exploration noise, minibatch order. Each one gives a *different policy*. | "trials", "independent agents", "seeds" | 8 independently trained agents per setting [[sahachaiseree2025-linear-fa-tsc]]; 6 trials [[turnau2025-jl-gat]] |
| **Evaluation / simulation seed** | Vehicle arrivals and driver behaviour in SUMO/VISSIM. The *same policy* is replayed on new traffic realisations. | "replications", "test episodes", "simulation runs" | 10 replicate VISSIM runs of one trained model [[kwesiga2025-mappo-north-ave]] |
| **Demand scenario** | The OD matrix, volume level, day, or incident pattern itself | "scenarios", "flows", "patterns", "days" | 7 real days [[li2024-d2tsc]]; 3 training plus 2 unseen OD patterns [[kwesiga2026-attention-xrl]] |

A "± std" over training seeds measures **how reliable the algorithm is**. A "± std" over evaluation seeds measures **how much the traffic varies for one fixed policy**. Most papers do not say which one they report. *(synthesis)*

**Patterns** *(synthesis)*

- **The CityFlow benchmark lineage trains and tests on the same flow file** (MPLight, GPLight, MAGSAC, SHLight). That is acceptable for comparing algorithms, but it says nothing about generalisation. [[han2023-rl-traffic-survey]] states the problem directly: *"only a few studies have isolated the training and testing environments."*
- **Held-out testing takes three forms:** held-out *networks* (TransferLight, Traffic-R1, CrossLight), held-out *dynamics* (Müller, PromptGAT, JL-GAT), and held-out *demand* (XRL, TREX Exp 2, Song).
- **No paper in the wiki uses a clean temporal split with held-out days.** D2TSC evaluates on the same 7 days it calibrated on. Meess spans the whole week but does not say whether test days were held out. Kwesiga MA-PPO trains and tests on one afternoon. If you have multiple days of TMC data, a day-level split is a gap you can fill cheaply.
- **3600 s episodes are the default.** Warm-up periods range from 100 s ([[nguyen2025-trex]]) to 300 s ([[sahachaiseree2025-linear-fa-tsc]]) to 15 min ([[kwesiga2025-mappo-north-ave]]). VISSIM practice uses the longest warm-up.
- **Training budget depends on phase count.** Kwesiga's 4-phase agents converged in about 300 episodes, but the 8-phase NEMA agent needed more than 1,000 [[kwesiga2025-mappo-north-ave]]. If you use the dual-ring NEMA action space, budget for the high end.

## 2. Seeds, checkpoint selection and statistics

| Paper | Training seeds | Eval runs per policy | Which checkpoint is reported | What "±" means | Statistical test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[sahachaiseree2025-linear-fa-tsc]] | **8** per setting (**16** per arrival pattern in later tests) | **30** seed-specific test episodes, no exploration; **identical seeds for every agent** | Converged weights | *s* across the 8 agents | **Student t-test + Mann–Whitney U, two-tailed** |
| [[nguyen2025-trex]] | **5** | 100 test episodes (Exp 2) | Train: mean of best 10 (DQN) / best 100 (PPO) consecutive episodes. Test: policy with best 10-episode ATT during training | Across seeds/episodes | None, but proposes **robustness metrics** (LSI, FPD, CR, AUC, RAUC, PDI) |
| [[li2024-d2tsc]] | **5** | Each of 7 days | "Best models" | Std across 5 seeds | None |
| [[da2024-prompt-to-transfer]] | **5** runs | n/a | n/a | Std across 5 runs | Correlation p-values (forward-model accuracy vs. gap) |
| [[turnau2025-jl-gat]] | **6** trials (2–3 in some ablations) | n/a | **Best epoch per trial selected by lowest ATT in E_real, i.e. on the test environment** | Sample std across 6 best epochs | None |
| [[gu2026-pi-elight]] | Not separated; 10 flows | 10 flow variants | Best searched program | Std across 10 flow variants | **Paired t-test** (vs. GPLight, e.g. t = 4.52, p = 0.001); IQR bands on curves |
| [[liao2025-symlight]] | Not separated; 10 flows | 10 flow variants | n/a | Std across 10 flows | **Wilcoxon rank-sum, α = 0.05, Bonferroni-corrected**; ↑/≈ marks in table |
| [[muller2023-bridging-reality-gap]] | Not reported | **5 episodes, same seeds for every agent** | n/a | Mean only | None |
| [[kwesiga2025-mappo-north-ave]] | **1** trained model | **10 replicate runs, unique seeds** | Final model | Box plots of 10 per-run averages | None ("significantly" used informally) *(inferred: no test reported)* |
| [[kwesiga2026-attention-xrl]] | 1 (implied) | **N = 10** runs per test scenario | Final model | Distributions over 10 runs | None |
| [[pei2025-dr-marl]] | 1 (implied) | **10 rollouts** per demand group | Final model | Mean over 10 rollouts | None |
| [[chen2025-roundabout-rl]] | "Identical random seeds" across methods; count unspecified | Unspecified "runs" | n/a | Std across runs | None |
| [[lu2025-rl-tsc-ac]] | Unspecified | Unspecified | "End of learning" | "/std" with unstated basis | None |
| [[zhou2025-c3dqn]] | **1** (SUMO default seed 23423) | — | **Last 100 training episodes** | Upper/lower bounds over those episodes | None |
| [[song2026-capacity-region-corridor]] | **1** (apparently) | Demand grid | **Highest-reward policy** | None | None |
| [[schmidt2024-transferlight]] | Not reported | 1 test run per benchmark | n/a | **Std over time steps within one 3600 s episode** *(inferred from caption: "over 3600 simulated time steps")* | None |
| [[sun2024-crosslight]] | Not reported | Avg of **10 eval epochs** on target city | After finetuning | None | None |
| MPLight | Not reported | Avg of **last 10 test episodes** | n/a | None | None |
| GPLight, [[shang2025-magsac]], [[shen2025-shlight]], [[zou2025-traffic-r1]] | Not reported | — / "about 10 episodes" (SHLight) | n/a | **Single numbers** | None |
| [[meess2024-first-steps]] | Not reported | Many weekly scenarios | n/a | **Mean and median** improvement, ±1 std, box plots across scenarios | None |
| RTOR study (`Quantifying the impacts of right-turn-on-red.pdf`, not RL) | — | **10 replications** with different starting seeds | — | — | **Paired t-test (95%) + three-way ANOVA**; 2 gridlocked seeds removed as outliers |

**Patterns** *(synthesis)*

1. **The norm is 5 training seeds (TREX, D2TSC, PromptGAT) and 10 evaluation runs (Kwesiga ×2, Pei, π-eLight/SymLight).** The upper bar in this wiki is 8–16 independently trained agents with 30 test episodes each [[sahachaiseree2025-linear-fa-tsc]].
2. **About half the papers report no variance at all.** That includes the most-cited CityFlow lineage (MPLight, GPLight, MAGSAC, Traffic-R1).
3. **Only 4 of about 25 papers run a significance test**: π-eLight (paired t), SymLight (Wilcoxon + Bonferroni), Sahachaiseree (t + Mann–Whitney), and the RTOR study (paired t + ANOVA). The two non-parametric tests suit RL, because seed distributions are often skewed or bimodal.
4. **Checkpoint selection is where most hidden optimism comes from.** The practices, from least to most optimistic:
   - fixed final model ([[kwesiga2025-mappo-north-ave]], [[kwesiga2026-attention-xrl]])
   - average of the last N episodes (MPLight, [[zhou2025-c3dqn]])
   - best N consecutive episodes ([[nguyen2025-trex]])
   - highest-reward policy ([[song2026-capacity-region-corridor]])
   - **best epoch chosen on the test environment** ([[turnau2025-jl-gat]]), which is test-set leakage

   [[nguyen2025-trex]] criticises this directly: *"many studies report best-case performance during training, which may not reflect reliability or robustness."* It also cites Henderson et al. 2018 on unstable deep-RL convergence.
5. **Common random numbers are the best low-cost practice.** Every controller is evaluated on the *same* traffic seeds ([[sahachaiseree2025-linear-fa-tsc]], [[muller2023-bridging-reality-gap]], [[chen2025-roundabout-rl]]). Differences between controllers are then *paired*, which removes the traffic noise from the comparison and makes paired tests valid.

## 3. Metrics used

| Metric | Used by | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Average travel time (ATT) | Nearly all CityFlow-lineage papers, [[turnau2025-jl-gat]], [[song2026-capacity-region-corridor]] | The default headline metric. It is biased when vehicles never finish, so pair it with throughput. |
| Throughput / finished trips | MPLight, π-eLight, SymLight, [[lu2025-rl-tsc-ac]] | Essential under oversaturation. [[lu2025-rl-tsc-ac]] shows loaded vs. finished vehicles diverging. |
| Queue length | [[nguyen2025-trex]], [[pei2025-dr-marl]], [[chen2025-roundabout-rl]], [[muller2023-bridging-reality-gap]] | Averaged per step or per intersection |
| Delay / waiting time | [[li2024-d2tsc]] (total intersection delay), [[sun2024-crosslight]], [[zou2025-traffic-r1]] | Definitions vary (delay vs. waiting time vs. time loss) |
| **Per-movement delay and corridor travel time by direction** | [[kwesiga2025-mappo-north-ave]], [[kwesiga2026-attention-xrl]] | Traffic-engineering style. It exposes reallocation (EB vs. WB) that aggregate ATT hides. |
| Pedestrian waiting time | [[muller2023-bridging-reality-gap]] | MAML improved vehicle metrics but made pedestrian waits worse |
| Performance Index (waiting + 60 × stops) | [[meess2024-first-steps]] | German guideline (Brilon) |
| Capacity region / zero-stop ratio | [[song2026-capacity-region-corridor]] | Compare ATT only where *all* controllers are stable |
| Learning-robustness metrics (LSI, FPD, CR, AUC, RAUC, PDI) | [[nguyen2025-trex]] | Score the *training process*, not just the endpoint |
| Learning cost vs. baseline during training | [[han2023-rl-traffic-survey]] | About 350 rush hours to beat ALINEA online |
| Sim-to-real gap Δψ = ψ_real − ψ_sim | [[da2024-prompt-to-transfer]], [[turnau2025-jl-gat]] | Gap metric, not absolute performance |

## 4. Answer to my question: evaluate the best seed, or average all 5?

**Short answer: report all 5. Keep "best seed" as a separate, clearly labelled *deployment* result, and pick that seed on validation data, never on test data.** *(synthesis)*

Why "train 5, report the best" causes trouble:

- **It reports a maximum, not an expectation.** Best-of-5 is an order statistic. It grows with the number of seeds even if the algorithm does not improve. A reviewer will read it as "on a lucky run". This is the same criticism [[nguyen2025-trex]] makes of best-case training reporting, and it is what [[turnau2025-jl-gat]]'s own wiki page flags as inflating results.
- **If "best" is decided on the same scenario you report, it is test-set leakage.** That is exactly the JL-GAT pattern (best epoch picked on E_real).
- **It hides instability.** For a deployment-oriented thesis, how often RL training fails is itself a finding. [[nguyen2025-trex]] built a whole metric suite around it, and the Centralized GAT in [[turnau2025-jl-gat]] collapsed to ±0.00 at 4×4. A best-of-5 report would hide all of this.

"Pick the best seed" does have a legitimate role: **in deployment you would ship one policy.** That is a model-selection step, and it is honest when done like an ML validation split:

```
demand days ──► TRAIN days      → train K seeds
             ├► VALIDATION days → pick checkpoint per seed + pick the "deployed" seed
             └► TEST days       → touched once, at the end
```

### Suggested protocol for the Atlanta NEMA / field-baseline study

| Step | Choice | Precedent in wiki |
|---|---|---|
| Demand split | **By day**: e.g. train on weeks 1–2, validate on week 3, test on week 4. Same TOD window; only 04:00–22:00 counts (night excluded, as in [[meess2024-first-steps]]). | None does it cleanly, so this is a contribution |
| Training seeds | **K = 5 minimum, 8–10 if compute allows** | 5 = [[nguyen2025-trex]], [[li2024-d2tsc]]; 8–16 = [[sahachaiseree2025-linear-fa-tsc]] |
| Checkpoint selection | Per seed, by **validation-day** performance (or simply the final checkpoint) | Avoid the [[turnau2025-jl-gat]] test-set pattern |
| Evaluation runs | Each policy × each test day × **M = 10 sim seeds** (more if variance is high) | 10 = [[kwesiga2025-mappo-north-ave]], [[kwesiga2026-attention-xrl]]; 30 = [[sahachaiseree2025-linear-fa-tsc]] |
| Common random numbers | **Same sim seeds for RL, field NEMA plan, MaxPressure, actuated** | [[sahachaiseree2025-linear-fa-tsc]], [[muller2023-bridging-reality-gap]] |
| Warm-up | 15 min (VISSIM-style) or ≥ 300 s, excluded from metrics | [[kwesiga2025-mappo-north-ave]], [[sahachaiseree2025-linear-fa-tsc]] |
| Headline number | **Mean across K seeds** (each seed's score = mean over its test episodes) **± 95% CI** using t with K−1 df, or bootstrap. Also plot all K seeds as dots. | Std across seeds = [[li2024-d2tsc]], [[turnau2025-jl-gat]] |
| Deployment number | The validation-selected seed on test days, with a CI over test episodes. Label it "selected policy". | Final-model reporting, [[kwesiga2025-mappo-north-ave]] |
| RL vs. baseline test | **Paired** test on per-episode differences (same day + seed): Wilcoxon signed-rank or paired t. Report the **effect size and CI of the difference**, not only p. | Paired t = [[gu2026-pi-elight]]; Wilcoxon = [[liao2025-symlight]] |
| RL vs. RL across seeds | Mann–Whitney U / Welch t on K seed-means. With K = 5 power is low, so lean on CIs. Bonferroni-correct if many comparisons. | [[sahachaiseree2025-linear-fa-tsc]], [[liao2025-symlight]] |
| Metrics | ATT **plus** throughput, per-movement delay, max queue / spillback. Check stability first: compare only where all controllers are under capacity. | [[kwesiga2025-mappo-north-ave]], [[song2026-capacity-region-corridor]] |
| Training-robustness | Optional: failure rate across seeds, final-vs-best gap (FPD) | [[nguyen2025-trex]] |

### Two variance levels: worth reporting both

- **Between-seed variance** answers "is the algorithm reliable?" Here the training seed is the unit of replication, n = K.
- **Within-seed variance across traffic seeds and days** answers "is this policy robust to traffic?" Here n = M × days.

Averaging everything into one ± hides which of the two is large. A two-row table, or a mixed model with seed as a random effect, separates them. *(synthesis; the mixed-model suggestion is general statistics, not from a wiki source)*

### Outside the wiki (general RL methodology, not in any source here)

- Henderson et al. 2018, *Deep RL that Matters*, is cited by [[nguyen2025-trex]] and [[meess2024-first-steps]] but not ingested. It is the standard reference showing that results flip with seed count and that "top-N of M seeds" reporting is misleading.
- Agarwal et al. 2021 (*rliable*) recommends interquartile mean and stratified-bootstrap CIs when the number of seeds is small (3–10). It is not in the wiki; ingest it if you want to cite it.

## Gaps the wiki can't answer well

- None of the papers justifies its seed count with a power analysis. The sample-size discussion from 2026-10-04 (log) was about the number of *intersections*, not seeds.
- No paper reports how much variance comes from training seeds vs. traffic seeds, so there is no prior for choosing K vs. M. A small pilot (e.g. K = 5, M = 10 on one site) would give you your own numbers.
